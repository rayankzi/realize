# 3 Ways to Actually Get Users for Your App or Startup

**Topic/Category:** Startup Marketing / Distribution Strategy
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** Teach indie developers and startup founders three actionable strategies to solve the distribution problem — getting their product in front of the right people instead of building in a vacuum.

---

## Executive Summary

The creator argues that the biggest mistake developers make during the current AI boom isn't building a bad product — it's having no distribution strategy. Most people reaching out to him have impressive apps but zero users, purely because they can't get their work in front of the right audience. He outlines three strategies: building a personal brand, studying distribution channels to meet your audience where they already are, and leading with unique value instead of a hard sell. He backs this up with his own experience — a 20-year-old university student who built a 4,000-person founder community and leverages that audience to validate and launch new tools.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Insight 1:** 99% of the time, a startup's failure to get users is a distribution problem, not a product problem. Developers need to spend as much energy on reaching people as they do on building features.
- **Insight 2:** Personal brand is the single highest-leverage distribution channel. It compounds over time — once you have an audience, launching new products becomes dramatically easier (e.g., 50 waitlist signups with zero promotion).
- **Insight 3:** Effective distribution leads with unique value (entertainment, insights, community resources) rather than a pitch. People follow opinionated creators and mission-driven companies, not product announcements.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Beginning (Hook):**
Opens with a strong warning — the "absolute worst thing" you can do at the peak of the AI bubble is spend months building a tool with no audience to launch it to. Immediately frames the problem as urgent and timely.

**Middle (Three Strategies):**

1. **Build Your Personal Brand**
   - Defined simply as "expanding the group of people who know who you are." Not about being flashy — just consistently putting yourself out there and making yourself known in your space.
   - Personal proof: Jack is 20, in university, works at a Toronto startup doing $9M+ ARR, built a 4,000-person founder community, and uses that audience to identify problems worth solving and launch tools directly to them.
   - Concrete example: Built a tool that takes vibe-coded UIs and makes them look professional. Put a waitlist link on his website without any promotion — got 50+ signups purely from existing distribution.

2. **Study Distribution Channels**
   - Work backwards from your target user. Identify where they already spend time.
   - Selling to businesses → Twitter, LinkedIn, Instagram (probably not TikTok).
   - Built a study tool for students → Instagram, TikTok, university campuses.
   - Mentions a list of websites where you can post your startup (Product Hunt ecosystem).

3. **Lead with Unique Value, Not a Pitch**
   - The best distribution doesn't start with "come buy my thing." It starts with "here's something genuinely useful for you."
   - Example: If you build an AI fishing app, don't just show use cases — create an email list of the best fishing hotspots. That's value people actually care about, and it doesn't feel like a pitch.
   - Become opinionated. People want to follow people and companies with a clear mission and point of view.
