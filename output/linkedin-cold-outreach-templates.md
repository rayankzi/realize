# Cold Outreach Templates for Breaking Into Tech

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Networking
**Primary Goal of the Post:** Provide specific, high-conversion cold outreach templates and rules for job seekers (especially those pivoting) to network effectively on LinkedIn and land roles in tech.

---

## Executive Summary

This carousel is a comprehensive guide for "non-techies" or career pivoters looking to break into startups or corporate tech roles. It addresses the common struggle of "desperate-sounding" outreach by providing six distinct templates tailored to different networking scenarios—ranging from asking for informational interviews to requesting referrals from warm contacts. The post emphasizes a value-first, low-friction approach, summarized by six core "rules" for LinkedIn outreach that prioritize genuine connection over transaction.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Pattern Breaking:** Successful outreach should "break the pattern" by not immediately asking for a job or a referral, but rather for a brief "learning conversation."
- **Context is King:** Always lead with who you are (role/background) and why you are reaching out to *them* specifically (referencing their work).
- **The "Low Friction" Ask:** Asking for a "quick chat this week or next" is an "easy yes," whereas "any advice?" or a direct job request is often ignored.
- **Referral Etiquette:** Only ask for referrals from people who know your work, and always ask for a chat first. Give the recipient an "explicit out" to reduce pressure.
- **Connection Strategy:** A connection request is not a DM; keep the initial note warm and light, saving the full ask for after they accept.

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

### 1. The Job-Specific Ask (Informational Interview)
> Hi [Name] — I'm a [role / background] with [X years] in [field], looking to move into [target role]. Your work on [specific thing] is exactly the kind of thing I'm trying to learn more about. Would you be open to a quick chat this week or next?

### 2. No Job Posted — Making the Ask (Hiring Manager/Team Lead)
> Hi [Name] — I'm a [role] with [X years] experience in [field], currently making the move into [target area]. I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific thing] — genuinely impressive. Not applying to a role, just want to be on your radar. Open to a quick chat this week or next?

### 3. Asking for a Referral (Warm Contact)
> Hey [Name] — loved your recent post on [topic], it really landed. I'm a [role] with [X years] in [field] and I saw [Company] is hiring for [role]. Would love to connect for a quick chat this week — and if you're comfortable, a referral would mean a lot. Zero pressure either way.

### 4. Asking Someone 10+ Years Ahead (Senior Leader)
> Hi [Name] — I'm a [role] with [X years] in [field], passionate about [specific interest]. Your work on [specific thing] is the clearest take on [topic] I've come across. Would you be open to a quick chat this week or next? I'd love to hear how you think about [specific area].

### 5. Just Genuinely Connecting (Non-Tech Freshers)
> Hi [Name] — I'm a [positioning / role / stage of life], interested in breaking into [target role] as I'm super passionate about [unique thing]. I loved your work on [specific thing / product / company]. Would you be willing to do a quick chat this week or next?

### 6. When You Have Something to Show (Project/Skill)
> Hi [Name] — I'm a [degree / background] grad passionate about [field]. I've been building [project / skill / thing] on the side and your work on [specific thing] has genuinely shaped how I think about it. Would love a quick chat this week or next — even 15 minutes would be huge.
