## Executive Summary

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Foundational Models:** The Black-Scholes model unlocked the modern derivatives market, while MPT (Modern Portfolio Theory) introduced the critical concept that risk is just as important as return.
- **Market Dynamics:** The Efficient Market Hypothesis suggests outperformance is difficult, yet the "Limits to Arbitrage" theory explains why markets don't always correct themselves.
- **Systematic vs. Unsystematic Risk:** CAPM teaches that expected returns are driven by systematic risk (beta) rather than individual asset noise.
- **Incentive Alignment:** Agency Theory explores the friction between managers and shareholders, explaining corporate inefficiencies.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**The 7 Foundational Papers:**

1.  **Black–Scholes (1973):** A closed-form model for pricing options using volatility, time, and interest rates.
2.  **Market Efficiency - Eugene Fama (1970):** Argues that markets reflect all available information, favoring passive indexing.
3.  **Modern Portfolio Theory - Harry Markowitz (1952):** Focuses on optimizing returns through diversification across uncorrelated assets.
4.  **CAPM - William Sharpe:** Links expected returns to systematic risk (beta).
5.  **Arbitrage Pricing Theory - Stephen Ross (1976):** Expands on CAPM by including multiple macro factors.
6.  **Agency Theory - Jensen & Meckling (1976):** Discusses misaligned incentives between managers and shareholders.
7.  **Limits to Arbitrage - Shleifer & Vishny (1997):** Explains why mispricings persist even in the presence of rational traders.
