# The Best Cover Letter Structure (From a Hiring Manager)

**Topic/Category:** Job Search / Career Tips
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To show job seekers a simple, effective cover letter structure that highlights skills with measurable proof — so they stop wasting time on generic, fluffy cover letters.

---

## Executive Summary

A hiring manager breaks down the best cover letter they've ever received, explaining why it works and how to replicate it. The key formula: a direct opener stating the role and fit, three core skills backed by specific metrics, and a brief closing with enthusiasm. The caption reinforces this structure and adds a mention of Reslink video pitches as a way to stand out. The entire approach is designed to take no more than 5–10 minutes per application.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Insight 1:** Skip generic openers like "I wanted to express my interest in…" — instead, state the job title, company, and why you're a fit in the first 2–3 sentences.
- **Insight 2:** Choose three key skills and pair each with a measurable result (numbers, percentages, timeframes). Vague claims like "I'm very operational" don't convince recruiters.
- **Insight 3:** Keep the cover letter simple, structured, and specific — spend no more than 5–10 minutes writing it before moving on to the next application.
- **Insight 4:** The caption introduces Reslink video pitches as a supplement, letting hiring managers see personality and communication style beyond a PDF.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Beginning (Hook):** The creator opens by stating their perspective as a hiring manager — they personally hate cover letters, but acknowledge some companies still require them. This sets up the value proposition: here's how to write one efficiently.

**Middle (Core Breakdown):**

1. **The Opener** — Write a simple, direct opening that mentions the job title, the company, and why you're a good fit. No fluff, no filler. This tells the recruiter immediately: "I know what you're looking for, and I can do it."

2. **Three Skills with Proof** — Instead of long paragraphs, pick three relevant skills and back each with a specific, measurable achievement:
   - Operations & process building → cut project timelines by 30%
   - Growth & go-to-market execution → drove 1,500 new users in the first 60 days
   - Founder support & strategy → contributed to a £250K pre-seed raise

3. **The Close** — End with a couple of lines expressing enthusiasm about the company. Keep it brief.

**End (Wrap-Up):** Reinforces the philosophy — simple, structured, specific — and offers to send the exact template to anyone who comments.
