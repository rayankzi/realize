# Why Cluely Almost Never Shows Their Product (And Why It Works)

**Topic/Category:** Startup Growth Strategy / Marketing
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To break down Cluely's counterintuitive content marketing strategy — targeting a broad audience rather than direct buyers — and extract a broader lesson about when top-of-funnel attention actually matters for startups.

---

## Executive Summary

Cluely, a fast-growing startup, has built its brand almost entirely through chaotic office content that rarely features its product. In this episode of "Startup Season," the creator spoke with Cluely's former CMO Daniel Min to understand why. The answer reveals a sophisticated understanding of funnel mechanics: Cluely targets students and young professionals broadly, knowing their actual buyers exist as a subset within that audience. More importantly, founder Roy Lee has a sharp framework for when virality actually translates to growth — and when it doesn't.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Broad audience targeting:** Cluely doesn't aim content at direct buyers. They cast a wide net (students, young professionals) and let the ideal customer self-select from within that larger pool.
- **Top-of-funnel ≠ growth:** Cluely has had videos with millions of views that produced almost no downloads — proving that going viral doesn't automatically translate to product growth.
- **Two separate jobs:** Top-of-funnel content (attention) and bottom-of-funnel content (conversion) serve completely different functions and must be understood as distinct levers.
- **Roy Lee's framework:** Top-of-funnel effort only truly matters in two scenarios: (1) when you need users to grow, or (2) when you need feedback on what to build. If you already know what to improve, more attention doesn't help much.
- **Strategic clarity over vanity metrics:** The best startups don't just chase attention — they understand *how* attention fits into their overall growth model before investing in it.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

- **Beginning (Hook):** Opens by acknowledging the viewer has likely already seen Cluely's content — and poses the question: why do they almost never show their product?
- **Middle (Core Argument):** Shares the insight from former CMO Daniel Min — the brand targets a wide audience of students and young professionals, with buyers being a smaller subset within that group. Distinguishes between top-of-funnel attention content and bottom-of-funnel conversion content, noting the two are completely different challenges.
- **End (Conclusion):** Delivers founder Roy Lee's specific mental model: top-of-funnel only matters when you need users or product feedback. Closes with the broader takeaway — smart startups understand how attention fits into growth, not just how to maximize it.
