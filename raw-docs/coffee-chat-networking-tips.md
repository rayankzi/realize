# How to Run a Great Coffee Chat (And Stop Bombing Them)

**Topic/Category:** Career Advice / Networking
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To help students and early-career professionals improve the quality of their networking coffee chats by addressing three common mistakes and emphasizing the importance of follow-ups.

---

## Executive Summary

The creator shares real feedback given to a student after a coffee chat that "was not good," framing it as a teachable moment. The core message is that coffee chats fail when you lack a clear goal, treat the conversation like a structured Q&A, and fail to follow up. Done right, coffee chats should feel like genuine conversations where you know what you want, you engage naturally, and you add value—even if just by keeping the other person in the loop on your progress.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Be explicit about your goal:** Walk into every coffee chat knowing what you want—a referral, industry insight, tips on breaking in—and state it clearly so the other person knows how to help you.
- **Have a conversation, not an interview:** Don't fire off a preset list of questions pulled from someone's LinkedIn. Listen, follow threads, and engage naturally.
- **Always add value in return:** The lowest bar is to make the other person feel their time wasn't wasted. Follow up with outcomes—who you met, what happened next.
- **Send the calendar invite yourself:** If you're asking for an intro, don't make them do the logistics. Take ownership and schedule it.
- **Follow-ups are non-negotiable:** Loop back after meetings and milestone moments. It turns a one-time conversation into a lasting relationship.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

- **Beginning (Hook):** Opens with a relatable and disarming hook—"I recently had a coffee chat with a student and it was not good." Immediately humanizes the topic and creates curiosity about what went wrong.
- **Middle (Core Advice):** Walks through three numbered mistakes/fixes:
  1. **Clarify what you want** — give the other person a clear way to help you.
  2. **Engage, don't interrogate** — avoid the "LinkedIn research dump" question list; have a real conversation.
  3. **Add value** — the minimum is a genuine follow-up that closes the loop (e.g., "I met the person you introduced me to and here's what happened").
- **End (Actionable Tip):** Emphasizes a concrete behavior: when requesting an intro, schedule the meeting yourself and send the calendar invite. Reinforces the principle of ownership and not wasting others' time.
