## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Research beats volume:** Target hiring managers and team leads — not random HR contacts — and understand their specific projects before writing a word.
- **Personalization in the opener is non-negotiable:** Referencing a recent launch or project signals genuine interest and immediately separates you from mass applicants.
- **Lead with relevance, not credentials:** Instead of restating your resume, highlight the specific skills that map to the role's actual challenges.
- **One clear, low-commitment ask:** A single "15-minute chat" request is far more effective than multiple options or an outright request for the job.
- **Template as lead magnet:** The creator uses Step 5 to funnel engaged viewers into a DM-based conversion ("DM 'KIT'"), capturing warm leads directly.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Hook:** Bold credibility claim — 12 cold emails → 7 interviews — contrasted with the failure of friends with better CVs.

**Step 1 — Research your target:**
Don't email random HR contacts. Identify the hiring manager or team lead and study their current projects and what they're looking for in a candidate.

**Step 2 — Personalize the opening line:**
Avoid the generic "I'm interested in your job opening." Instead, reference something specific: *"I saw your team's recent launch on XYZ product and was impressed by your innovative approach to ABC."*

**Step 3 — Show targeted value:**
Don't just list skills from your resume. Highlight specific experiences that directly address the challenges or needs listed in the role.

**Step 4 — Make a simple, frictionless ask:**
Don't beg for the job or present multiple options. Use one clean request: *"Would you be open to a 15-minute chat to explore how I can contribute to your team?"*
