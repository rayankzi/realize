# Document Everything: How to Own Your Narrative at Work

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Workplace Strategy
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To teach professionals how to systematically document their contributions, wins, and agreements at work so they can protect themselves and accelerate their career growth during performance reviews.

---

## Executive Summary

Most professionals rely on their manager's memory to validate their contributions — and that's a losing strategy. This reel argues that if you document your work, wins, and workplace conversations, you control the narrative at review time. The creator lays out a four-part documentation system: maintaining a "brag sheet," saving positive communications, tracking 1:1 conversations, and writing down goals in writing at the start of the year. The throughline: "If it's not documented, it didn't happen."

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Your manager's memory is not your ally:** Leadership and HR won't proactively track your contributions — you have to do it yourself.
- **Quantify and date every win:** A running "brag sheet" with metrics gives you data at review time, while your manager only has vague impressions.
- **Save praise and approvals:** Screenshots of Slack messages and forwarded emails create a proof trail that silences anyone who questions your impact.
- **Recap 1:1s in writing:** A short follow-up email after every manager meeting creates a paper trail that protects you if priorities shift or blame is assigned.
- **Lock in goals early:** Getting your manager's expectations in writing at the start of the year removes ambiguity and prevents moving goalposts at review time.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Hook:** Opens with the provocative claim that your manager, HR, and leadership won't remember or track what you did — framing documentation as self-protection.

**Step 1 — Brag Sheet:**
Start a running document. Log every project delivered, problem solved, and metric improved. Date and quantify each entry. At review time, you show up with data; your manager shows up with guesses.

**Step 2 — Save Communications:**
Collect praise from clients, positive peer feedback, and leadership approvals. Screenshot Slack threads. Forward emails to a personal folder. When contributions are questioned, produce proof.

**Step 3 — Recap Every 1:1:**
After every one-on-one with your manager, send a brief email confirming what was discussed and agreed. This creates a paper trail and protects against shifting blame or changed priorities.

**Step 4 — Write Down Goals in January:**
At the start of the performance cycle, document exactly what your manager expects of you — ideally with their sign-off. At review time, you can demonstrate point-by-point delivery against those goals.

**Closing:** The creator reinforces the core message — "Memory fades. Documentation doesn't. If it's not documented, it didn't happen." — and drives to a CTA.
