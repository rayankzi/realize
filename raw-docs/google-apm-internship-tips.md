# How to Land a Competitive Tech Internship: Tips from a Google APM

**Topic/Category:** Career Advice / Tech Recruiting
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To provide actionable tips for landing competitive tech internships based on the creator's personal experience interning as an Associate Product Manager (APM) at Google.

---

## Executive Summary

This video features a former Google Associate Product Manager (APM) intern sharing her top three strategies for navigating a tough recruiting season. After being rejected once and succeeding the following year, she emphasizes the importance of quantitative impact on resumes, a judgment-focused approach to behavioral interviews, and the necessity of practicing interview responses out loud.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Quantitative Resume Impact:** Every single line on a resume must demonstrate impact using numbers. This makes contributions stand out and ensures no space is wasted.
- **Behavioral Interviews are About Thinking, Not Just Stories:** Interviewers care less about the "backstory" and more about your judgment, decisions, and approach to problem-solving.
- **Active Interview Practice:** Prepping by reading or writing is insufficient. Practicing out loud and recording yourself is essential to identify the difference between sounding rehearsed and sounding natural.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

The creator breaks down the recruitment process into three critical phases:

1.  **Phase 1: The Resume (Before the Interview)**
    - Focus on "impact" for every single line.
    - Include at least one quantitative value per line to show exactly what was done and how it affected the company or customers.
2.  **Phase 2: The Interview (Behavioral Questions)**
    - Transition from storytelling to demonstrating thinking and judgment.
    - When asked about a challenge, briefly describe the situation but focus heavily on what you did, why you made certain decisions, and what you learned.
3.  **Phase 3: Preparation (The Practice)**
    - Do not practice in your head or just on paper.
    - Practice speaking out loud to bridge the gap between thinking and vocalizing.
    - Record yourself and watch it back to refine your delivery.
