# 3 Steps to Brainstorm a Winning Hackathon Idea

**Topic/Category:** Tech / Hackathon Strategy
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** Teach aspiring hackathon participants a structured three-step brainstorming method to come up with a strong, unique, and compelling project idea.

---

## Executive Summary

This reel provides a concise, actionable framework for generating hackathon ideas. The creator emphasizes starting with distraction-free thinking (specifically putting away ChatGPT), then validating uniqueness feature-by-feature, and finally framing the idea with genuine enthusiasm and a compelling narrative. The caption reinforces each step with additional detail and ends with a shoutout to a community called "Tech Cadets" for people looking to build projects over the summer.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Insight 1:** Put away AI tools like ChatGPT and set a 30-minute distraction-free timer to brainstorm independently. Focus on real-world problems, ideas that excite you, and existing technologies you can improve.
- **Insight 2:** Validate your idea by mapping out the user experience feature-by-feature, researching existing solutions, and developing a concrete technical integration path — this tests whether your idea is truly unique.
- **Insight 3:** Frame your idea with a compelling, creative narrative. You should be genuinely enthusiastic about it. The creator cites "Home Hero" as an example — a project framing individuals with disabilities as superheroes with strengths, powers, and weaknesses.
- **Insight 4:** This brainstorming method is universal — it applies to any domain, not just human-centric ideas. Whether it's crypto, developer tools, or technical innovation, the same three steps work.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Beginning (Hook):** The creator opens with a direct address — "So you want to start going to hackathons and you're trying to come up with an idea" — immediately qualifying the target audience and promising a solution.

**Middle (Core Framework):**

1. **Step 1 — Independent Brainstorming:** Put away ChatGPT. Set a 30-minute timer. Think without distractions. Ask yourself three guiding questions:
   - What problem in the world can be solved?
   - What ideas genuinely excite you?
   - What existing technologies can you improve?

2. **Step 2 — Feature-by-Feature Uniqueness Test:** Map out the application from the user's perspective. Research existing solutions. Develop a technical integration path to ensure your approach is differentiated.

3. **Step 3 — Frame the Idea:** Create a compelling name or core concept around the idea. The creator stresses being genuinely compelled — not just technically sound, but emotionally invested. Example given: "Home Hero," a project centered on empowering individuals with disabilities by framing them as superheroes.

**End (Community Plug & CTA):** The creator broadens the method's applicability beyond human-centric ideas to any technical domain, then promotes a Discord community called "Tech Cadets" (searchable on LinkedIn as "Techadettes") for people who want to build projects over the summer, especially those without internships.
