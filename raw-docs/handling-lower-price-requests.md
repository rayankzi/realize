# Mastering the Lower Price Request: Reassurance and Value

**Topic/Category:** Sales Strategy / Negotiation
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To provide a step-by-step framework for handling clients who ask for a lower price without immediately devaluing the service or product.

---

## Executive Summary

When a client asks for a lower price, it's often a signal that they are ready to buy but need reassurance regarding the value they're receiving. This reel outlines a high-status approach to negotiation: instead of immediately discounting, the salesperson should use pattern-interrupting questions to uncover the real concern, pivot to value-based incentives (like loyalty bonuses), or offer flexible payment terms to secure a "yes" while maintaining the integrity of the original price.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Lower Price = Ready to Buy:** A price objection is often a buying signal wrapped in a request for reassurance.
- **Avoid Immediate Discounts:** Instantly agreeing to a lower price signals "low status" and suggests the original price was arbitrary or inflated.
- **The Unexpected Question:** Asking "How much of a discount were you hoping for?" disrupts the prospect's expectations and softens their defenses.
- **Isolate the Concern:** Distinguish between a genuine budget constraint ("total price") and a need for better value perception ("something else you're unsure about").
- **Trade Value for Commitment:** If offering a discount, tie it to a longer commitment (e.g., 6 months) to secure a long-term relationship.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

- **The Hook:** Framing the price request as a signal of buying intent rather than a rejection.
- **Step 1: The Disruption:** Ask the client exactly how much of a discount they were expecting.
- **Step 2: The Acknowledgment & Clarification:** Validate their response and ask if the hesitation is about the total cost or the perceived value.
- **Step 3: The Close (Value Focus):** If value is the issue, offer a loyalty bonus in exchange for a longer contract term.
- **Step 4: The Close (Budget Focus):** If the total price is the issue, offer a split payment plan (e.g., 50% down) to lower the barrier to entry.
