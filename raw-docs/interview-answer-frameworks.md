## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Structure your "Introduce Yourself" around three anchors:** affirmations (gratitude), status quo (who you are now), and goals (where you're heading).
- **Strengths & Weaknesses must be strategic:** Pick weaknesses that won't disqualify you from the role — avoid clichés like "poor time management" or "bad team communication."
- **"Why us?" answers should be specific:** Reference actual programs, internships, or values from the institution/company, not generic praise.
- **Differentiate without putting others down:** Acknowledge the competition respectfully, then spotlight one specific achievement that maps directly to what they're looking for.
- **Conflict answers follow a mature, forward-looking arc:** Situation → your calm action → what you learned → what you'll do differently.
- **Never say "I have no questions":** Flip it into genuine curiosity with smart, role-aware questions that show investment.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

**Slide 1 (Cover):** Title card — "How to answer interviews + get in" — sets a confident, aspirational tone.

**Slide 2 — Introduce Yourself:**
Framework: Affirmations → Status Quo → Goals
- Affirmation: Express excitement to be there and gratitude for the invite.
- Status quo: State your name, your current situation (degree, university, major), and a current role/activity.
- Goals: Connect your future ambitions to the opportunity you're interviewing for.

**Slide 3 — Strengths & Weaknesses:**
- Strengths to highlight: leadership, critical thinking, language/communication, adaptability, people skills.
- Weaknesses to use (safe ones): overthinking details, handling tasks solo before asking for help, being too independent.
- Hard rule: **Do NOT** mention poor time management or bad team communication.

**Slide 4 — Why Do You Want to Join Us?**
- Reference a specific program, internship, or initiative at the organization.
- Connect your past experience to why you're committed to continuing that growth *here*.
- Mention how your inclusion benefits *them*: leadership development, diverse perspective, competitive achievements.

**Slide 5 — What Makes You Different?**
- Acknowledge the competitive field without disparaging other candidates.
- Pivot to one specific, quantifiable achievement in a relevant area that directly aligns with the program's goals.

**Slide 6 — Tell Me About a Time You Handled Conflict / Leadership / Failure:**
Four-part structure:
1. Explain the conflict maturely (no gossip/blame).
2. Describe your calm, ideally leadership-oriented action.
3. Acknowledge what you could have done better.
4. Share what you learned and how you'll apply it going forward.

**Slide 7 — Ask Questions Back (Instead of "I have no questions"):**
- What is the work ethic like?
- What is the organisation's goal for this period?
- What are the challenges my role might face?
- What is the best candidate like in your opinion?

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

**Introduce Yourself — Sample Script:**

> "Thank you again for inviting me to this interview. I am very excited to get to know the university/internship/etc. Allow me to introduce myself. My name is [Name], you can call me [Nickname]. I am a double-degree student studying law in both [University A] and [University B], with a big interest in [AREA]. I am currently conducting an internship/competition/research/etc. in the [AREA] I mentioned earlier. My goals for the future would be to study here / because / work here / because."

---

**Strengths & Weaknesses — Framework:**

> "Strengths: leadership, critical thinking, language = communications, adaptability, people skills.
> Weaknesses: overthinking details, trying to handle all the tasks by myself, being too independent with tasks before asking for help."
>
> DO NOT mention poor time management and horrible communication with the team!!!!!

---

**Why Do You Want to Join Us? — Framework:**

> "I am particularly interested in the dual-degree program."
> "I am interested in the available internship in [WORKPLACE]."
> "Given my previous experience in [XXX], I am committed to continuing my passion through this program."
> "Beyond my academic/work history, I am aiming to further develop my leadership skills at [PLACE]."
> "I hope to contribute by bringing a fresh perspective shaped by my diverse background."
> "I aim to contribute to this university by bringing home trophies from competitions."

---

**What Makes You Different? — Framework:**

> "I do notice that this is a competitive recruitment with high-achieving candidates."
> "What sets me apart is my [SPECIFIC ACHIEVEMENT] in [AREA] that produces [XX]. I believe this aligns with the program!"

---

**Conflict/Leadership/Failure — Sample Answer:**

> "In my previous internship, we had a miscommunication regarding one section of our research. We were supposed to include 10 sources, not 5. I calmed down the situation and told my teammates not to panic, and decided to ask for an extended deadline. From this experience, I learned to be more meticulous with our projects."

---

**Questions to Ask the Interviewer:**

> - What is the work ethic like?
> - What is the organisation's goal for this period?
> - What are the challenges my role might face?
> - What is the best candidate like in your opinion?
