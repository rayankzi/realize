# How to Network as an Introvert: 3 Game-Changing Tips

**Topic/Category:** Networking & Career / Personal Development
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To reframe networking for introverts by shifting away from transactional, professional-first thinking toward genuine human connection — with 3 concrete, low-pressure tactics.

---

## Executive Summary

Posted by @advicewithjean, this carousel challenges the conventional approach to networking and offers a refreshing, introvert-friendly perspective. Rather than treating networking as a formal career exercise, the creator encourages showing up as a real person — reaching out casually, asking meaningful questions, and giving generously without expecting reciprocity. The content is grounded in personal experience and speaks directly to people who find traditional networking exhausting or inauthentic.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Authenticity beats professionalism:** Networking doesn't have to start with "let's catch up professionally" — a meme or random memory is often more memorable.
- **Questions create connection:** Swapping "what do you do?" for open-ended personal questions makes conversations feel human, not transactional.
- **Generosity is the long game:** Making intros, sharing resources, and offering time with no strings attached builds lasting goodwill and reputation.
- **Introverts have a natural edge:** The low-key, thoughtful style of outreach described here plays to introverted strengths — depth, intentionality, and genuine care.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

**Slide 1 (Cover):** Title card — "How to network when you're an introvert" — set against a photo of the creator with a group of friends at a social gathering, establishing warmth and relatability.

**Slide 2 — Tip 1: Reach out when someone crosses your mind**
- Don't reach out to "catch up professionally" — just say hey.
- Send a random memory, a funny meme, or something like "Sending you X. You'd like this!"
- Key message: *"That stuff sticks way more than you think."*

**Slide 3 — Tip 2: Ask better questions**
- Avoid: "What do you do?"
- Instead try: "What's been the highlight of your week?" / "What brings you here?" / "Which Netflix show are you binging?"
- Key message: *"People want to know you OUTSIDE of your professional setting."*

**Slide 4 — Tip 3: Give without expecting anything back**
- Make that intro. Share that tip. Offer your time.
- Share helpful resources: *"This book changed how I think about X. Want me to send you the link?"*
- Key message: *"People remember your kindness (always)."*
