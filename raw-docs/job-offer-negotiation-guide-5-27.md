# Negotiation 101: Master Your Job Offer

**Topic/Category:** Career / Professional Development / Negotiation
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To empower individuals (specifically mentioning Asians) to negotiate their job offers effectively by providing core principles, specific benchmarks, and actionable templates.

---

## Executive Summary

This post challenges the cultural narrative of "being grateful and not making waves" by asserting that negotiation is essential, even if you are satisfied with the initial offer. It breaks down five key principles for navigating job offers, provides a specific 15% salary increase benchmark, and offers verbatim scripts to handle recruiter pushback and communicate counteroffers professionally.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Negotiation is Mandatory:** Always negotiate, even if the offer seems fair. It's about proving your worth and gaining practice.
- **The 15% Benchmark:** Start by adding 15% to the base salary offer as a standard starting point for a counteroffer.
- **Precision Matters:** Use non-round numbers (e.g., $172,500 instead of $170,000) to signal that your request is backed by thorough research.
- **Package Deal Integrity:** Never agree to individual components of an offer piecemeal. Only agree to the full package once all elements are aligned.
- **Beyond Base Salary:** If the salary is "final," pivot to other levers like signing bonuses, equity (RSUs), PTO, remote flexibility, or relocation stipends.

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

### Counteroffer Email Template
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks so much for the offer, so pumped to get going with [Company name].
>
> I took some time to look at the full package alongside what the market looks like for similar roles at [industry/company size, example: "Fortune 100 tech companies"] in [location]. With my [1-2 specific things: your YOE, a relevant achievement, a specialized skill], I'd love to see if we can get the base closer to [$X].
>
> I want to make this work and ensure we're aligned before I sign. Happy to hop on a call if that's easier to talk through.
>
> Looking forward to it,
> [You]

### Handling Recruiter Piecemeal Tactics
> "I'd prefer to agree to the offer as one package and I'll wait for you to get an answer back on my salary request before agreeing the other components of the offer"
