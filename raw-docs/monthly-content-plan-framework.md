**Topic/Category:** Content Creation / Social Media Strategy

## Executive Summary

The creator walks through a rapid-fire, 5-step content planning system presented as a dialogue between a coach and a student. ## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Posting Cadence Matters:** Start by choosing a posting frequency—easy, moderate, or hard—so the plan is realistic and sustainable (example: "moderate" = Monday–Friday).
- **Schedule Keeps You Accountable:** The specific days you post matter less than having a committed schedule; it's a self-accountability tool.
- **Niche + Subtopics = Content Pillars:** Writing out your niche and 3–5 subtopics creates the strategic foundation everything else builds on.
- **Content Type Graph:** Visualizing content types in a graph format reveals what types resonate most with your audience and helps balance your content mix.
- **Plan Before You Create:** Mapping topics and types onto your calendar before creating anything eliminates decision fatigue and prevents blank-slate paralysis.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Beginning — Hook:** Opens with an urgent constraint ("I only have 45 minutes to plan an entire month of content") to grab attention and signal a fast, actionable video.

**Middle — The 5-Step Framework:**

1. **Posting Cadence** — Choose a posting pace: easy, moderate, or hard. The demonstrator picks *moderate*.
2. **Posting Schedule** — Commit to specific days (e.g., Monday–Friday). Days themselves are less important than the consistency they enforce.
3. **Content Topics** — Write down your niche and 3–5 subtopics related to it. This forms your content pillars.
4. **Content Types** — Create a visual graph of content formats and fill it in to see which types you'll use most.
5. **Plan the Month** — Combine your favorite topics and types from the graph to map out a full month of content.

**End — Payoff + CTA:** The "student" reacts with amazement ("Whoa, that's incredible"), validating the simplicity of the system. The creator teases a bonus offer—1,000 viral hook templates—to drive comments and engagement.
