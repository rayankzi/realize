## ☕ Networking: Turning Social Awkwardness into Opportunity

If you find yourself being socially awkward, remember that **networking is about making the other person feel seen, heard, and valued**, not about "performing." Start by asking better questions to invite connection and stories.

### 🎯 7 High-Impact Networking Questions

- **1. "What brought you here today?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Opens the door without pressure; invites them to share their intention or story.
- **2. "What's one thing you're working on right now that excites you?"**
  - _Why it works:_ People light up when they talk about passion projects.
- **3. "How did you get into your line of work?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Taps into their origin story, which people love sharing.
- **4. "What's been your biggest win this year?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Creates a positive emotional spike; you'll be remembered as someone who uplifted them.
- **5. "Who's someone in your industry you really admire?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Great for opening up shared interests and values.
- **6. "What's a challenge you're facing right now?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Builds trust; vulnerability invites connection and often leads to opportunities.
- **7. "How can I support what you're doing?"**
  - _Why it works:_ Nobody asks this; it leaves a lasting impact by showing you're there to give, not just take.

## 💼 Interview Hack: Answering Skill Gaps

It's okay to apply for a role even if you don't meet every requirement! You just need a strong answer for the skills you don't yet have.

### 🔑 The High-Impact Response

This response works because it highlights soft skills that companies love: **curiosity, adaptability, and resilience.**

- **Acknowledge the gap, but focus on past success:**
  > “Not having every qualification isn’t new to me. In my current (or past) role, I’ve had to learn new skills quickly. [Add your short example.]"
- **Highlight the three core skills:**
  - **Curiosity:** "I research, ask questions, and look for learning resources."
  - **Adaptability:** "I adjust fast to new people, tools, and responsibilities."
  - **Resilience:** "I stay determined, learn from mistakes, and keep improving."
- **The close:** "These qualities help me learn fast and make an impact early on."
- **_Crucial Tip:_** Use the same three skills, but **personalize your story** to make it authentic.

## ☕ Coffee Chat Playbook: A Step-by-Step Plan

Avoid wasting networking opportunities by over-talking about yourself or asking generic questions. Follow this step-by-step plan:

| **Step**   | **Time**     | **Action**                                                                     | **Example Script**                                                                                                        |
| ---------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **STEP 1** | 0–5 min      | **Start Strong:** Open with thanks and something personal.                     | _"Thanks again for meeting! I’ve really been looking forward to hearing more about your journey.”_                        |
| **STEP 2** | 5–10 min     | **Ask Intentional Questions:** Use 3-5 high-impact, open-ended questions.      | _“What’s a challenge you’re working through right now?”_ or _“What was your first big break?”_                            |

### The Golden Rule

The most effective way to land a job is by speaking with someone who already works there. To maximize these opportunities, follow these three rules:

- **Don't Skip the Small Talk**
  - **The Why:** The first five minutes are crucial. Professionals are often stressed or between meetings; jumping straight into technical questions feels transactional.
  - **The How:** warm them up by asking about their day, hobbies, or favorites.
  - **The Goal:** Create a human connection so they think, "I’d love to work with this person."
- **Go in With a Clear "Ask"**
  - **The Why:** Having a conversation with no direction leaves a weak final impression.
  - **The How:** Treat the meeting like a 30-minute sales call. Determine beforehand what the person can actually offer (e.g., an analyst might not be able to hire you, but they can refer you to someone who can).
  - **The Goal:** Pitch yourself toward a specific, defined outcome.
- **Always Use a Template**
  - **The Why:** "Winging it" leads to poor results. You need to help them help you.
  - **The How:** Keep a document open on your screen during the call.
  - **The Content:** Include your elevator pitch (word-for-word), 10 pre-written questions, and the specific outcomes you want by the end of the chat.

Here is the summary of XML prompting, organized into neat bullet points:

### What is XML Prompting?

- **The Definition:** The practice of enclosing your prompt instructions within clear tags (e.g., `<role>`, `<context>`, `<task>`) to visually separate different components.
- **The Analogy:** It is like handing someone a structured form to fill out, rather than shouting instructions over a noisy phone line.

### Why It Is Effective

- **Better Adherence:** Because the structure is crystal-clear, the model follows directions more accurately and makes fewer errors.
- **Programmatic Parsing:** It eliminates "messy string hacking." You can easily extract data from the model's response if it replies using tags (e.g., `<reasoning>` vs. `<answer>`).
- **Easier Debugging:** Issues are easier to spot when the model separates its internal logic from the final output.

### How to Use It

- **The Structure:** Instead of a long paragraph, break it down:
  - **Messy:** "Act as an expert..."
  - **Clean:** `<role>Expert</role> <task>Generate text</task>`
- **The Shortcut:** You don't need to write the tags manually. You can simply ask the AI (Claude or GPT) to "Write this as an XML prompt with role, context, and task," and it will generate the structure for you.
- **Best For:** Complex AI agents and automated workflows.

Here is the summary of the networking strategy, broken down into four clear steps:

### 1. Targeted Alumni Research

- **The Method:** Use LinkedIn to filter by your school and identify alumni in specific roles (Sales, Trading, Structuring).
- **The Focus:** Target people who were once in your shoes rather than chasing "big names." This shared background makes your message personal and increases the response rate.

### 2. Short and Genuine Outreach

- **The Rule:** Do not ask for a job or a referral in the first message.
- **The Hook:** Ask for insight (e.g., "I’d love to hear about your experience at your desk").
- **The Psychology:** People respond to curiosity, not desperation.

### 3. High-Effort Preparation

- **The Homework:** Before the call, research their specific bank, their desk, and relevant market news.
- **The Conversation:** Ask questions that demonstrate you understand their world (e.g., asking about market intuition or specific floor skills). This yields information you cannot find in online courses.

### 4. Strategic Follow-Up

- **The Action:** Send a short thank-you note immediately after the chat, summarizing exactly what you learned.
- **The Payoff:** Over time, these contacts can convert into sources for mock interviews, internal recommendations, and job alerts.
