# How a PhD AI Researcher Actually Uses Claude (With Real Prompts)

**Topic/Category:** AI Tools, Research Productivity, PhD Life
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To share exactly how Caitlin, a PhD researcher in AI, uses Claude as a research assistant — covering four specific use cases with real prompts she uses daily.

---

## Executive Summary

Caitlin, a PhD researcher in AI who has "tried all the tools," breaks down her four go-to Claude workflows: identifying knowledge gaps in her arguments, compiling research via web search, extracting per-paper summaries, and performing deep research on new ideas. The post emphasizes that Claude serves as a research *support* tool, not a replacement for developing research skills, and shares the actual prompts she uses to get results.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Identifying Knowledge Gaps:** Claude evaluates arguments for confirmation bias and surfaces papers that might have been missed — critical for ensuring balanced, thorough literature reviews.
- **Compiling Research:** Claude's web search function can aggregate research papers and news articles into a single Google Doc, complete with short summaries, saving significant time daily.
- **Extracting Insights from Papers:** Rather than reading every paper in full, Claude reads each and produces concise 1–3 sentence summaries of methods, findings, and limitations — enabling faster triage of what's actually relevant.
- **Deep Research on New Ideas:** When encountering a new concept, Claude can generate a structured report summarizing the idea alongside supporting and opposing key publications, giving a solid launchpad for deeper self-directed learning.
- **Ethical framing:** Caitlin acknowledges deep research support is "controversial" for PhD work and frames the line clearly — Claude digests information so she can dive deeper herself, not do the thinking for her.
