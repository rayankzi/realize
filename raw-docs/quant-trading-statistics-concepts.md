## Executive Summary

This reel breaks down five fundamental statistical concepts that quantitative traders rely on daily: mean and variance for position sizing, stationarity for strategy validation, autocorrelation for signal direction, volatility clustering for dynamic risk management, and the central limit theorem as the foundational business model behind systematic trading. ## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Insight 1:** Every trade begins with two numbers — expected return (mean) and the spread of outcomes (variance). High return with unacceptable variance gets sized down or rejected outright.
- **Insight 2:** Stationarity is the make-or-break assumption: if the statistical properties of a signal change over time, strategies built on historical data will fail. Testing for stationarity before deployment is non-negotiable.
- **Insight 3:** All systematic strategies are fundamentally bets on autocorrelation structure — momentum exploits positive autocorrelation, mean reversion exploits negative autocorrelation.
- **Insight 4:** Static position sizing is dangerous because volatility clusters — high-vol begets high-vol, calm begets calm. Quants model this dynamically and scale exposure in real time.
- **Insight 5:** The central limit theorem is the entire business model of systematic quant trading: individual trades are unpredictable, but thousands of small positive-EV trades converge to consistent returns.
