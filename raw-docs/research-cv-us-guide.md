# How to Build a Research CV (for US Positions)

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Medical Research
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To provide a clean, comprehensive template and actionable tips for medical students and professionals building a research-focused CV for positions in the United States.

---

## Executive Summary

This guide breaks down the essential components of a research CV, specifically tailored for US-based medical and academic positions. It emphasizes a structured narrative of one's academic journey, highlighting research impact through clear, concise sections and professional formatting. The carousel serves as a step-by-step visual template, from contact information to specialized skills and certifications.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **The CV as a Narrative:** View your CV as your "academic story"—it should be easy to read but memorable to the reviewer.
- **Impact-Oriented:** Don't just list roles; focus on tools, techniques, and outcomes (e.g., "Assisted in manuscript writing," "Outcome: published").
- **Conciseness is Critical:** Tailor the content to the specific role and focus only on relevant experiences.
- **Visual Clarity:** A well-structured, error-free document demonstrates professional attention to detail.
- **Specific Section Inclusion:** Essential sections include Research Interests, Education, Research Experience, Publications, Presentations, Grants/Awards, and Skills.

## Detailed Breakdown / Slide-by-Slide

- **Slide 1: Introduction:** Title slide with core tips: Tailor to the job, highlight research impact, use clear sections, keep it clean/concise, and proofread.
- **Slide 2: Header & Education:** 
  - Header: Full name, Email, Phone, LinkedIn, and research profiles (Google Scholar, PubMed, ResearchGate).
  - Education: Degree (MBBS/MD), University, Country, Dates, relevant coursework, and additional degrees.
- **Slide 3: Research Experience:** How to list Research Assistant/Intern roles. Includes institution/lab name, PI name, topic, data tasks, writing assistance, and outcomes.
- **Slide 4: Publications:** Formatting for authors, title, journal name, and year. Specifically includes a "Role" line to clarify individual contribution (data collection, analysis, etc.) and a link (DOI/URL).
- **Slide 5: Presentations:** Breakdown for Poster vs. Oral presentations, including title, event name, and year.
- **Slide 6: Honors / Awards:** Categories for research-related awards, grants, poster awards, best paper awards, and Dean's List.
- **Slide 7: Clinical Experience:** Template for clinical work/internships/observerships, including hospital name, location, and key exposure/responsibilities.
- **Slide 8: Skills:** Categorized into Software (SPSS, R, Python, EndNote/Zotero), Research (study design, systematic review, IRB submission), and Clinical (patient interaction, documentation).
- **Slide 9: Certifications & Interests:** 
  - Certifications: RCOP workshops, CITI Training, etc.
  - Interests (Optional): Brief, humanizing details (reading, fitness, travel).
- **Slide 10: Final Tips:** Summary tips: Be concise/relevant, use clear/specific details, and keep it well-structured/error-free.

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

### Research Experience Template
> **Research Assistant / Intern - [Institution / Lab Name]**
> [Month Year] – [Month Year]
> - Worked on [topic] under [PI name]
> - Collected/analyzed data
> - Assisted in manuscript writing
> - Outcome: [submitted/published]

### Publication Entry Template
> **Author(s). Title. Journal Name. Year**
> Role: [data collection / analysis / writing]
> Link: [PubMed / DOI / URL]

### Software Skills List
> SPSS, R, Excel, Python (basic), EndNote / Zotero

### Research Skills List
> Literature review, data collection, IRB submission, study design, systematic review, meta-analysis
