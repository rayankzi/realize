# 10 Strategic Questions to Ask at the End of a Research Interview

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Academia / Economics Research
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To provide economics RA and pre-doc candidates with strategic questions to ask at the end of an interview to signal curiosity, evaluate fit, and leave a lasting impression.

---

## Executive Summary

This post outlines ten high-impact questions specifically designed for candidates applying for Research Assistant (RA) and pre-doc positions, particularly in economics. By treating the interview as a two-way conversation, candidates can gain deeper insights into the role's reality, the mentorship style of the Principal Investigator (PI), and the long-term career prospects the position offers.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Strategic Inquiry:** Asking the right questions is a strategic move that signals intellectual maturity and thorough preparation.
- **Role Reality Check:** Questions about a "typical week" and project stages help clarify if the work is mundane data cleaning or higher-level analysis.
- **Mentorship Evaluation:** Understanding feedback loops and the track record of previous RAs helps assess if the environment is supportive of growth.
- **Intellectual Engagement:** Seeking opportunities for writing, attending seminars, and developing original research questions signals ambition and genuine interest.
- **Proactive Feedback:** A final personal question allows candidates to address any reservations the interviewer might have in real-time.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

### About the Research and Your Role
1. **Q1: What does a typical week look like for the RA on this project?** (Grounds the role in reality: data cleaning vs. regressions vs. lit searches).
2. **Q2: At what stage is the project right now, and what will the RA's contribution be to moving it forward?** (Identifies where you enter the pipeline and what skills you'll build).
3. **Q3: Will I be expected to contribute to writing — literature reviews, coding documentation, or any part of the draft?** (Determines the level of substantive intellectual engagement).

### About Mentorship and Feedback
4. **Q4: How do you typically give feedback to RAs, is it ad hoc, through regular check-ins, or something else?** (Clarifies the communication and guidance structure).
5. **Q5: Have previous RAs on this project gone on to PhD programmes or pre-doc positions? What did they find most useful about the experience?** (Assesses the role as a career launchpad).

### About Expectations and Working Style
6. **Q6: What does success look like in the first three months for an RA on this project?** (Forces a concrete performance bar and shows proactive thinking).
7. **Q7: Are there specific software tools or coding conventions the team uses that I should be familiar with or prepared to learn?** (Signals professionalism and readiness to hit the ground running).

### About the Broader Research Environment
8. **Q8: Will I have the opportunity to attend seminars, reading groups, or department events as part of the position?** (Checks for a mentorship-oriented environment).
9. **Q9: Is there any scope for the RA to develop their own research questions or propose extensions to the project?** (Signals intellectual ambition and curiosity).

### A Final, Personal Question
10. **Q10: Is there anything about my application or our conversation today that you would want me to address before we wrap up?** (Directly addresses reservations and demonstrates self-awareness).
