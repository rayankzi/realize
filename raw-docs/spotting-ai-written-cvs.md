# 5 Ways Recruiters Spot an AI-Written CV

**Topic/Category:** Career Advice / Recruitment Tips
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To help job seekers identify and fix the telltale signs that their CV was written by AI, so it doesn't get dismissed by recruiters.

---

## Executive Summary

Suze, a recruiter with over 15 years of experience and former marketing leader at Microsoft, breaks down the five dead giveaways that a CV has been written by AI. She emphasises that using AI to refine, spell-check, or polish a CV is perfectly fine — but relying on it to write the whole thing produces generic, bland results that recruiters immediately recognise. The core message: keep your CV simple, real, human, and interesting so you stand out from the flood of AI-generated applications.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Insight 1:** Recruiters are actively trained to spot AI-generated content in CVs — it's not flying under the radar.
- **Insight 2:** The biggest problem with AI-written CVs isn't that they're "wrong" — it's that they're vague, wordy, and fail to communicate actual achievements or impact.
- **Insight 3:** Small formatting artefacts (bold words, em dashes, excessive hyphens, American English in UK applications) are easy fixes that immediately make a CV look more authentic.
- **Insight 4:** Using AI as an editing assistant is encouraged; using it as the primary author is what gets flagged.
- **Insight 5:** A strong personal summary should be concise and results-driven (e.g., "led marketing campaigns from brief to delivery, drove X% uplift") rather than filled with abstract buzzwords.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Beginning — The Hook & Credibility:**
Suze opens with a direct question — "How to tell if a CV has been written by AI" — and immediately establishes authority by citing her 15+ years in recruitment and her background at Microsoft. She sets a balanced tone: AI assistance is fine, but full AI authorship is a problem.

**Middle — The Five Giveaways:**

1. **Overuse of Bold Text:** AI-generated professional summaries often bold descriptive words like "strong," "qualified," and "experienced." Fix: remove unnecessary bold formatting.

2. **Em Dashes (—):** AI loves using em dashes (long hyphens) throughout content. Fix: scan the CV and replace or remove them.

3. **Excessive Hyphens:** Similar to em dashes, AI substitutes hyphens where commas or full stops would be more natural. Fix: replace hyphens with appropriate punctuation.

4. **American English in UK Applications:** AI defaults to American spellings (e.g., "optimized" with a Z instead of "optimised" with an S). Fix: switch to UK English unless the role is international.

5. **AI Waffle in the Personal Summary:** The most damaging giveaway. AI produces long, meaningless sentences stuffed with buzzwords that say nothing concrete. Suze reads a real example: *"I'm a highly motivated professional with a proven track record of leveraging strategic marketing initiatives to drive impactful outcomes across multiple stakeholder environments."* She then rewrites it to something human and specific.

**End — The Rewrite & Takeaway:**
Suze demonstrates what the AI-written summary should actually say: that the person led marketing campaigns from brief to delivery, worked with senior stakeholders, and drove measurable business uplift. She closes with the mantra: "Keep it simple, keep it real, keep it human."
