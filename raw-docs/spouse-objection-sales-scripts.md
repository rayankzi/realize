# Handling the "Spouse" Sales Objection: The She Sells Academy Method

**Topic/Category:** Sales Coaching / High-Ticket Sales
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To provide high-ticket closers with psychological frameworks and scripts to overcome the common "I need to talk to my spouse" objection.

## Executive Summary

Shelby Sapp, founder of She Sells Academy, shares advanced techniques for handling the "spouse" objection in high-ticket sales. The core philosophy centers on shifting the prospect's mindset from seeking permission to presenting a solution. By using empathy-based "flip-flop" scenarios and third-party perspectives, Sapp demonstrates how to help prospects realize that they already have the support they think they need to seek, effectively moving them toward a closing decision.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Solution-Oriented Framing:** Prospects often fail because they present the decision to their spouse as a "problem" (indecision) rather than a "solution" (a trusted path to a goal). Spouses are far more likely to support a confident solution.
- **The Reciprocity of Support (The Flip-Flop):** Most people who say they need to check with a spouse would immediately support that same spouse if the roles were reversed. Highlighting this reciprocity reduces the perceived barrier.
- **Externalizing the Decision:** When emotions cloud judgment, asking the prospect what they would advise a best friend to do helps them access an unbiased, logical perspective.
- **Mindset of the Closer:** The goal isn't to bypass the spouse, but to empower the prospect to lead the conversation with their spouse from a place of conviction rather than doubt.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

The video follows an informational breakdown of three distinct objection-handling frameworks:

1.  **The "Problem vs. Solution" Reframe:**
    *   **The Problem:** Approaching a spouse with "I want to do this, but I don't know."
    *   **The Solution:** Approaching with "I found someone who can solve my problem, I trust them, and I'd like your support."
2.  **The "Flip-Flop" Technique:**
    *   Sapp asks the prospect if they would support their spouse in a mirrored situation.
    *   When the prospect says "yes," she establishes that their spouse would likely offer the same support.
3.  **The Third-Party / Best Friend Perspective:**
    *   Shifting the context to a friend going through the same situation.
    *   If the prospect would tell their friend to "do it," they should apply that same logic to themselves.
4.  **Conclusion:** A decisive "Let's do it" to lead the prospect into the next step.

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

> **Script: The Problem vs. Solution Reframe**
> "You're either going to come to her though with a problem or a solution. You know, a problem of, 'hey, honey, I really want to do this, but I don't know if I should' or, 'hey, honey, I found somebody that can really help me solve my problem that I've been wanting. I really trust it. And I thank you for your support.' Which one do you think would go over better with your wife?"

> **Script: The Flip Flop**
> "If instead, instead of it being me and you talking, what if it was me and your wife, you know, and she came to me and she's saying, 'I'm going through this, this, and that. And I finally found someone that I think can solve my problem. And I really want to do it, but I want to check with my husband first.' Would you support her in making that decision? You probably would, right? So is it fair to say that she'd probably support you in the same way that you support her?"

> **Script: The Best Friend / Third Party Perspective**
> "Let's say it's your best friend, you know, and she's sitting here going through this, going through the same thoughts. Would you tell her to do it? You know, probably would, right? Well, is it fair to say other people would probably tell you to do it too? ... What would you tell that person to do right now? Because you're saying to me that you really want to do it. You'd probably say to do it, right?"
