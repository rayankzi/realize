# Overcoming the "I Need to Think About It" Sales Objection

**Topic/Category:** Sales Strategy / Objection Handling
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To teach sales professionals how to handle the "I need to think about it" objection by challenging the prospect's decision-making mentality rather than just looking for a hidden objection.

---

## Executive Summary

This video provides a tactical script for reframing a prospect's hesitation on a sales call. Instead of treating "I need to think about it" as a simple smokescreen, the creator suggests attacking the prospect's underlying decision-making process. By getting the prospect to acknowledge that their past way of thinking led to their current (unsatisfactory) situation, the salesperson can guide them toward making a new decision that aligns with their desired future goals.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Reframing the Objection:** "I need to think about it" often means the prospect's identity or mentality hasn't changed yet.
- **Mental Shift over Objection Hunting:** Don't just look for what's "behind" the objection; directly challenge the mentality that created the hesitation.
- **The Cost of Inaction:** Use the prospect's current dissatisfaction (e.g., poor health, low energy) to highlight that their current way of making decisions is the problem.
- **Future Pacing:** Get the prospect to visualize the negative consequences of maintaining their current mentality for another two days, weeks, or years.
- **Empowering the Decision:** End by asking the prospect what decision *they* feel they need to make to achieve their goals, making the choice theirs rather than a forced sale.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

1.  **Acknowledge & Validate:** Start by saying the hesitation isn't a problem.
2.  **Question the Process:** Ask if "thinking about it" is how they usually make decisions.
3.  **Connect Decisions to Results:** Get agreement that their decision-making style shapes their life results.
4.  **Audit Current Results:** Ask if they are happy with their current situation (e.g., "Are you 100% happy with where you're at in terms of health?").
5.  **Identify the Conflict:** Ask if they are willing to let the same "way of thinking" that got them into their current mess dictate their future.
6.  **Highlight the Future Consequences:** Ask what happens if they keep making decisions this way for the next few months or years.
7.  **The Closing Question:** Ask, "So what decision do you feel like you need to make, man?"

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

### The "Thinking About It" Reframe Script

> **Salesperson:** Well, that’s not a problem. Is that like, I guess, usually how you make decisions?
>
> **Prospect:** Oh, well, yeah, of course.
>
> **Salesperson:** And would you agree that I guess like the way we make decisions kind of like, I guess, shapes the results we get in life?
>
> **Prospect:** Well, yeah, I guess that makes sense. Yeah, yeah, I agree.
>
> **Salesperson:** So between you and I, man, like if you're selling a fitness offer, are you 100% happy with, I guess, where you're at in terms of like your health and fitness?
>
> **Prospect:** Well, no, not at all, man. I mean, I'm having all these issues. I can't work out with my kids. I can't do this. I don't have a lot of energy.
>
> **Salesperson:** Okay, so then, and again, I ask this because I care, but like, are you willing to allow that same way of thinking that got you to this position where you don't have a lot of energy? You're not able to play sports with your kids. To dictate your fitness, your health, your mobility, your energy, and everything else moving forward.
>
> **Prospect:** Well, no, of course, I'm not willing to let that happen, man.
>
> **Salesperson:** Well, why not, man? Because between you and I, like that's what most people do. Because what do you think would happen if you allowed that same way of thinking that got you to this position, the same way of making decisions, to dictate your future, your energy, your fitness, your health, everything else moving forward for the next two days, two weeks, two months, even two years?
>
> **Prospect:** Oh, well, I'd be even more out of shape and I would be terrible. I wouldn't have any energy. I would have to quit working earlier.
>
> **Salesperson:** So what decision do you feel like you need to make, man? To put yourself in the best possible position so when your head hits the pillow tonight, you know you did everything in your power to get more energy, to get into better shape for you, for your family, for your relationships, and everything else. Because that's what I want for you.
