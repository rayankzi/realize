# How to Become a Top 1% CS Major: A 4-Step Guide

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Computer Science
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To provide a practical, non-traditional roadmap for CS students to stand out and secure high-level roles in big tech by focusing on infrastructure, networking, and soft skills.

---

## Executive Summary

This post outlines a strategic 4-step framework for CS majors to transition from having "no internships or job offers" to becoming a mid-level software engineer in big tech. The author emphasizes that coding alone is no longer sufficient, advocating for a deep dive into infrastructure (DevOps, Linux, Home Labs), campus involvement, and a personality-driven approach to interviewing.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Beyond Coding:** Modern engineering roles require knowledge of DevOps, storage systems, and Linux—not just application-level code.
- **The Value of Community:** Campus engineering clubs (like SJSU's SCE) provide essential skills, leadership opportunities, and networking that are difficult to replicate in the classroom.
- **Interviewing is a "Vibe Check":** Soft skills and personality account for a significant portion of interview success. Standing out through humor and rapport can be as important as technical performance.
- **Mastering Infrastructure:** Deep technical knowledge in "complex" areas like SSL/TLS certificate management and advanced Linux internals (syscalls, networking, system administration) provides a massive competitive advantage.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

- **Step 1: Expand Beyond Code:**
    - Learn DevOps and underlying storage mechanics.
    - Master Linux.
    - **Actionable Project:** Build a "Home Lab" from scratch to learn infrastructure hands-on.
- **Step 2: Campus Engagement:**
    - Join engineering clubs (Example: SCE at SJSU).
    - Pursue leadership roles (e.g., VP of a club).
    - Leverage club connections for internship leads and project experience.
- **Step 3: Master the Interview Dynamics:**
    - Treat interviews as personality tests/vibe checks.
    - Use humor to stand out and build rapport with tired recruiters/hiring managers.
    - Be adaptable (e.g., the author's anecdote of taking their own interview notes for a tired recruiter).
- **Step 4: Deep Technical Specialization:**
    - **HTTPS & Certificates:** Learn to create CA bundles and automate renewals with `cert-manager`.
    - **Linux Internals:** Focus on deep topics: `inodes`, disk partitioning, `syscalls`, `inotify`, `netplan`, `journalctl`, `systemctl`, and `dmesg`.
