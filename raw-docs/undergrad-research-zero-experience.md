# How to Secure Undergraduate Research with Zero Experience

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Academic Research
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To provide a practical strategy for undergraduate students to land research positions, even if they lack prior experience or specific technical qualifications.

---

## Executive Summary

The creator shares their personal journey of landing a position at their university's hardware lab despite having "zero lab experience" and no prior knowledge of FPGAs (the lab's focus). The core message is that securing research is a numbers game combined with having a solid technical foundation. By attending university research showcases (like "SOURCE") and applying to every opportunity regardless of perceived qualifications, students can increase their chances of finding that "one chance" that leads to a position.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **The Numbers Game:** Success in landing research often depends on volume. The creator's strategy was to "scan every single interest QR code" at a research showcase.
- **Qualified vs. Quantifiable:** You don't need to meet every single requirement. You need enough of a technical foundation (e.g., past coding or related AI experience) that shows you have the capacity to learn the specific niche of the lab.
- **Undergraduate Expectations:** Professors don't expect undergraduates to be experts. They look for students who can learn fast, show up, and not waste time.
- **Foundational Assets:** Having a solid, ready-to-go resume with any relevant technical experience is crucial for when that "one lab" gets back to you.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

1. **The Event:** Attend university research showcases or events where labs present their work (e.g., "SOURCE").
2. **The Action:** Scan every interest QR code and fill out every form, regardless of whether you feel qualified or if it's directly in your field.
3. **The Philosophy:** Operate on the principle that "it only takes one."
4. **The Interview/Selection:** Leverage existing technical skills (like coding or general AI knowledge) to prove you can handle the learning curve of a new field (like FPGA hardware).
5. **The Mindset:** Realize that "checking off enough boxes" to show potential is more important than checking off every single box.
