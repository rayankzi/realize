# How to Secure Undergraduate Research Opportunities

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Academic Research / STEM
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To provide undergraduate students with actionable strategies for finding and securing research opportunities across various fields, specifically highlighting cold emailing, internal school programs, and external programs.

---

## Executive Summary

This post by Pia Makinang (Pia Dela Cruz) outlines a multi-faceted approach to obtaining undergraduate research positions. Drawing from her own diverse experiences—ranging from amphibian field work to aerospace medicine—Pia emphasizes three main pillars: cold emailing, leveraging university-specific programs, and applying to large-scale external research initiatives. The post is presented in a "maximalist collage" style, combining dense information with a playful, colorful aesthetic.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Cold Emailing as a Strategic "Gamble":** While not guaranteed, cold emailing is a powerful tool for meeting future advisors and practicing self-advocacy.
- **Leverage Existing Connections:** The best cold emails often mention a referral or a specific shared interest (e.g., a professor's suggestion or a niche field like frog biodiversity).
- **University Resources:** Many institutions offer specific apprenticeship programs or research for academic credit and stipends that students often overlook.
- **Broad Application Strategy for STEM:** For fields like aerospace and biology, look toward established national programs like DOE SULI, ORNL RSI, and NSF REU.
- **Persistence and Uniqueness:** Everyone's journey is different; if one method (like cold emailing) doesn't work, pivot to institutional or external programs.
