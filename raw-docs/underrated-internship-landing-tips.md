# 4 Tips to Land Your Next Internship

**Topic/Category:** Career Development / Internship Search
**Primary Goal of the Post:** To provide a strategic framework for students to secure high-tier internships in competitive fields like tech and consulting.

---

## Executive Summary

This carousel outlines a four-step system for landing internships, leveraging the creator's personal success in securing roles at prestigious firms like BCG, Siemens, and KPMG. The strategy focuses on perfecting application documents, building a robust experience base, treating the search as a volume game supported by networking, and balancing technical and behavioral interview preparation.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Document Perfection:** A CV must be a single page, free of typos and alignment errors, containing only relevant information. Cover letters should be personalized for each company rather than generic.
- **Experience Gaps:** If a resume is empty, it should be filled with personal projects, student clubs, TA/RA roles, certifications, and hackathons before applying.
- **The Numbers Game:** Securing one offer typically requires 20–30 applications. However, networking and "warm intros" are significantly more effective than cold applications.
- **Balanced Interview Prep:** Behavioral and "personal" questions (culture fit, motivation) are as critical as technical ones. A 70/30 split between technical and behavioral prep is recommended.
- **Strategic Resources:** Specific platforms like CaseCoach, PrepLounge, and LeetCode are recommended for specialized prep in consulting and tech.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

- **Slide 1: Hook:** "4 Tips to land your next internship" featuring the creator's impressive internship history (BCG, Capgemini, Yttrium, Siemens, KPMG).
- **Slide 2: Get your documents right:** 
    - **CV:** One page, no typos, no alignment errors. Cut irrelevant info.
    - **Cover Letter:** Don't rewrite every time, but have one personalized part. Avoid obvious ChatGPT one-shots; mention the company by name and give a specific reason for wanting the role.
- **Slide 3: Build experience worth listing:** If the CV is empty, add:
    - Personal projects, student clubs, TA/RA roles, volunteering/extracurriculars, good grades, certifications/courses, hackathons, and open-source contributions.
- **Slide 4: Play the numbers:** 
    - Expect to apply to 20–30 positions for every offer.
    - Stack "unfair advantages": Networking (LinkedIn, alumni), coffee chats, and warm intros.
- **Slide 5: Interview prep:**
    - Don't overprep technicals and underprep personal questions.
    - 70/30 prep time split (Technical/Personal).
