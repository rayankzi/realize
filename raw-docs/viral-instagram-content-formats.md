## Executive Summary

This carousel breaks down the structural "secret sauce" of viral Instagram content, moving the focus away from just the "idea" and toward the "format." By testing over 50 content styles with AI, the creator identifies three specific frameworks—Micro-Masterclasses, Strategy Drops, and Lessons Learned—that predictably trigger saves and shares. The post positions Claude as a "content bestie" to help creators reverse-engineer these viral structures through dedicated prompting.

## Key Insights & Main Ideas

- **Format > Idea:** Viral success is often more about how information is structured than the unique brilliance of the underlying idea.
- **High-Engagement Metrics:** The goal of these formats is to maximize "Saves" (utility) and "Shares" (relatability/authority), leading to "Predictable Growth."
- **AI-Powered Workflow:** Claude is used to bridge the gap between having a topic and having a high-performing carousel structure.
- **Repeatable Systems:** Content creation should move from "winging it" to a structured process: Intent -> Research -> Data Injection -> Refinement -> Learning.

## Detailed Breakdown / Step-by-Step

The carousel follows an informational progression, introducing three specific frameworks:

### Format #1: Micro-Masterclass Carousels
- **Focus:** Teach one specific thing deeply in 5-7 slides.
- **Examples:** "How to write hooks that stop the scroll," "3 Manychat keywords that actually convert."
- **Structure:** 
    - Slide 1: Strong hook.
    - Slides 2-7: Breakdown of the topic into 5-6 easy, step-by-step tips.

### Format #2: Behind-The-Scenes Strategy Drops
- **Focus:** Show the process behind a result (the "how"), not just the final outcome.
- **Examples:** "How I booked 5 clients from 1 reel," "The 3 emails that made me $10k last month."
- **Structure:** Hook -> Context/Challenge -> 3-5 Key Steps -> Tools Used -> Result/Data -> Lessons -> CTA.

### Format #3: The Lessons-Learned Post
- **Focus:** Share vulnerable, practical insights from failures or milestones.
- **Examples:** "3 lessons from launching a digital product that flopped," "What I wish I knew before hitting 10k followers."
- **Structure:** Bold hook -> Describe mistake/belief -> What actually happened -> Takeaway/Mindset shift -> CTA for reflection.

### The AI Method (Bonus Tips)
- **Method #1:** Use Claude to set up a daily schedule of hooks and ideas.
- **Method #2:** Use Claude to analyze viral content structures in seconds.
- **Method #3:** Generate infographics and diagrams for visual learners.

## Verbatim Templates & Scripts

The post provides three distinct Claude prompts for content generation:

### Prompt for Micro-Masterclass (Format #1)
> You are a content strategist who specializes in Instagram carousels for [niche].
> 
> Create a tutorial-style Instagram carousel that solves one specific pain point for someone with zero prior knowledge.
> 
> Structure:
> - Slide 1: A strong hook that stops the scroll
> - Slides 2-7: [Break] down the topic into 5-6 easy, step-by-step tips

### Prompt for Strategy Drop (Format #2)
> You are a growth strategist revealing a campaign that achieved [specific result].
> 
> Create a 7-slide carousel for [platform] that breaks down exactly how the result was achieved — step-by-step.
> 
> Structure:
> 1. Strong hook (call out the result or pain point)
> 2. Brief context (what the challenge was)
> 3. 3-5 key steps taken
> 4. Tools or platforms used
> 5. Specific result or data
> 6. 1-2 lessons learned
> 7. CTA (e.g. "Steal this strategy")

### Prompt for Lessons-Learned (Format #3)
> Write a 6-7 slide carousel titled "3 Things I Wish I Knew Before [milestone or challenge]."
> 
> Make it honest, practical, and experience-based. Start with a bold, relatable hook.
> 
> Then, for each point:
> - Describe the belief or mistake
> - What actually happened
> - The takeaway or mindset shift
> 
> Use simple, conversational language. Prioritize emotional connection and real insight over generic advice. End with a CTA that invites reflection, comments, or DMs (e.g. "Which one hits hardest?" or "Send this to someone").
