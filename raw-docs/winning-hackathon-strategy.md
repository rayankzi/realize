# Winning Hackathons: A 24-Hour Strategic Breakdown

**Topic/Category:** Hackathons, Software Engineering, Career Advice
**Primary Goal of the Reel:** To provide a strategic framework for winning hackathons by prioritizing idea validation, user journey mapping, and presentation quality over excessive coding.

---

## Executive Summary

The creator shares a "hot take": winning hackathons is not hard if you stop over-focusing on code and start focusing on three key characteristics: the idea, the user journey, and the presentation. Drawing from his own experience of placing or winning at every hackathon he's attended (including Harvard and SDSU), he provides a precise 24-hour schedule designed to maximize the chances of walking away with prize money.

## Core Insights & Takeaways

- **Coding is Not the Priority:** The biggest mistake teams make is spending too much time coding and not enough time on the strategy and "why" behind the project.
- **Brainstorm Religiously:** Don't settle for the first decent idea. Validate your concept with judges early to ensure alignment with the track's goals.
- **Demo-Driven Development:** If a feature isn't going to be shown in the technical demo, it doesn't matter. Focus exclusively on the core user flow that solves the problem.
- **Communication Beats Technicality:** Technically superior projects frequently lose to teams that can better communicate their project's value and problem-solving impact.

## Narrative & Step-by-Step Breakdown

The video follows a chronological 24-hour plan:

1. **Hours 1 through 4: The Idea**
   - Focus on brainstorming and being "hypercynical" about the idea's validity.
   - **Validation Step:** Take your idea to a panel judge early to ensure you are solving a problem they actually care about.
2. **Hours 4 through 16: The User Journey**
   - Understand that you aren't building a production-ready product.
   - Focus on the "core flow" of the application.
   - Rule of thumb: If it's not in the demo, skip it.
3. **Hours 16 through 24: The Presentation**
   - Dedicate the final 8 hours to how you will communicate the project.
   - Focus on the "why" and how the idea matters to the judges.
